ALEFIT’s personalization is still too generic. I want you to improve how answers change the actual training—not just add more questions or insert someone’s sport into a generic workout.

Inspect the current onboarding, plan-generation logic, exercise database, and saved athlete profiles. Then implement genuine personalization using the existing architecture.

Keep the interface clean. Do not redesign unrelated pages.

1. MAKE EVERY ANSWER MATTER

The training plan must meaningfully respond to:
- Primary sport or general fitness.
- Specific goals and their priority.
- Position, event, or discipline when relevant.
- Training experience and recent consistency.
- Available equipment.
- Available training days and session duration.
- Existing sports practices, games, or competitions.
- Relevant age considerations and movement limitations.
- Actual workout history and readiness feedback.

For each input, identify what programming decision it changes. If a question does not affect the plan, remove it or implement its purpose.

2. USE SHORT, BRANCHING ONBOARDING

Do not give everyone the same long questionnaire.

Start with approximately six core screens. Reveal a small number of relevant follow-ups based on previous answers.

Examples:

Basketball:
- Main priority: jumping, acceleration, strength, conditioning, or general athleticism.
- Optional position.
- Existing practice and game days.
- In-season or off-season.

Running:
- Target distance or general running goal.
- Recent weekly running volume.
- Longest comfortable recent run.
- Optional race date.
- Current pace only if known; do not require it.

Strength/muscle:
- Primary goal.
- Recent lifting consistency.
- Familiarity with basic movements.
- Available equipment.
- Optional recent working weights, never a required max-strength test.

General fitness:
- Desired improvement.
- Current activity level.
- Preferred activities.
- Practical schedule.

Let users select “Not sure.” Explain how the app will start conservatively and learn from feedback.

Do not pretend every sport has expert-specific support. Use an honest general athletic-development fallback where necessary.

3. PERSONALIZE THE ACTUAL PROGRAM

Build a structured athlete profile and use it to determine:
- Weekly training frequency.
- Session placement around practices and competitions.
- Training emphasis.
- Exercise and drill selection.
- Sets, reps, time, distance, rest, and effort.
- Starting difficulty.
- Exercise substitutions.
- Progression and recovery.

Distinguish goals properly:
- Strength should affect loading, rest, and progression.
- Muscle development should affect volume and exercise selection.
- Power should emphasize quality, appropriate recovery, and avoiding excessive fatigue.
- Endurance should affect conditioning structure and workload.
- Sport preparation should consider existing sport demands.

Do not simply give every athlete the same bodybuilding split.

Account for total workload: a hard sports practice is not a rest day just because it was not an ALEFIT workout.

4. CREATE A COHERENT MULTIWEEK PLAN

The plan should be more than unrelated daily workouts.

Build a sensible progression:
- An appropriate starting week.
- Repeatable key exercises so progress can be measured.
- Gradual changes based on completion and difficulty.
- Recovery adjustments when needed.
- Sport-season or event considerations where relevant.

Do not change exercises randomly every day to make the app seem intelligent.

Upcoming sessions may adapt. Completed workouts must remain an accurate record of what the user did.

5. USE A STRUCTURED EXERCISE DATABASE

Exercises and drills should include metadata such as:
- Movement pattern.
- Equipment required.
- Difficulty and prerequisites.
- Relevant training qualities.
- Impact and fatigue considerations.
- Appropriate alternatives.
- Prescription parameters.

Use that information to filter and select exercises.

Equipment availability, schedule, and session-duration limits should be enforced—not treated as suggestions.

Do not treat simple keyword matching as reliable injury screening. Avoid diagnosing or prescribing rehabilitation. Provide conservative options and recommend qualified guidance where appropriate.

6. SHOW THE PERSONALIZATION BEFORE SIGNUP

The preview should make the connection between answers and programming obvious.

Show:
- Their training focus.
- Their weekly schedule.
- A useful first workout.
- Recovery days.
- Three concise explanations of specific decisions.

For example:
“You have basketball practice Tuesday and Thursday, so we placed your additional strength sessions on Monday and Saturday.”

Only show explanations that accurately describe decisions the planner actually made.

Include a “Change answers” option that updates the real plan.

Then invite account creation:
“Save my plan and start today.”

Save the reviewed profile and starting plan—not a different generic plan after signup.

7. LEARN FROM COMPLETED TRAINING

After a session, collect minimal useful feedback:
- Completed, partially completed, or skipped.
- Too easy, appropriate, or too hard.
- Relevant logged weights, reps, time, or distance.
- Optional pain or discomfort flag.

Use this information to:
- Progress gradually when appropriate.
- Hold or reduce difficulty when necessary.
- Adjust unrealistic session lengths.
- Suggest substitutions for unsuitable exercises.
- Reorganize missed sessions without cramming everything into the next day.

Do not automatically progress a user merely because a calendar week passed.

8. ADAPT TO TODAY WITHOUT LOSING THE PLAN

Use a quick check-in:
- Energy.
- Soreness.
- Time available.
- Relevant additional sport activity.

If the user only has 20 minutes instead of 45, prioritize the important work and reduce the session accordingly.

If fatigue is elevated, consider lighter work or recovery.

Explain significant changes briefly. Do not rewrite the entire program after one low-energy day.

Pain must not be treated as normal soreness or something to push through.

9. MAKE REST DAYS PERSONALIZED TOO

Recovery guidance should reflect recent workload and upcoming training.

Offer appropriate options such as complete rest, easy movement, or gentle mobility. Do not make recovery another intense session or imply that optional activity is mandatory.

10. DO NOT FAKE AI INTELLIGENCE

A chatbot or an AI-generated paragraph is not sufficient personalization.

Use structured programming rules and validation. If an LLM is used:
- Call it server-side.
- Validate its output.
- Enforce equipment, time, workload, and safety constraints.
- Provide a reliable fallback.
- Do not expose API keys.
- Do not add paid services without approval.

Clearly distinguish implemented rules from actual model capabilities.

11. PROVE THAT PERSONALIZATION WORKS

Create test profiles with meaningfully different needs, including:

A. Beginner basketball athlete with dumbbells, two available strength days, and three weekly practices.

B. Experienced basketball athlete with full-gym access, four available training days, and an off-season power goal.

C. Beginner runner preparing for a first 5K with low recent running volume.

D. Experienced lifter focused on muscle growth with limited 30-minute sessions.

E. General-fitness user training at home with bodyweight only.

For each profile, verify differences in:
- Weekly structure.
- Exercises.
- Volume and effort.
- Conditioning.
- Recovery.
- Progression.

Also test changing one input at a time:
- Removing equipment changes incompatible exercises.
- Reducing session time reduces actual prescribed work.
- Adding a game changes the surrounding workload where appropriate.
- Repeated “too hard” feedback prevents automatic progression.
- Missed sessions do not produce unsafe catch-up schedules.

Do not merely test whether a plan was returned. Test whether it obeys the profile.
