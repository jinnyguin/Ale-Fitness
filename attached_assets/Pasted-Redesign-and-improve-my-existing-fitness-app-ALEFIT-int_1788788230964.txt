Redesign and improve my existing fitness app, ALEFIT, into a polished, mobile-first personalized training app.

This is an existing project. Inspect its code, authentication, database, routes, and deployment configuration before making changes. Preserve existing accounts and useful functionality. Implement the app—not just a landing page, mockup, or development plan.

PRODUCT DIRECTION

ALEFIT should feel like a focused personal training app, with Runna as the main inspiration for organization and user experience—but for multiple sports and fitness goals, not just running.

Learn from Runna’s clear training schedule and daily guidance, Ladder’s workout-first experience, and Fitbod’s personalization.

Do not copy their branding, assets, wording, or exact interface. Keep ALEFIT’s identity.

The core experience should be:
Answer a few questions → preview a personalized plan → create an account → know exactly what to do today → complete training → receive useful adjustments.

The personalized plan is the product. Do not organize the experience around a collection of unrelated calculators or 20 generic programs.

DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

Make this feel like a premium fitness app on an iPhone, not a crowded fitness website.

- Clean, restrained, athletic design.
- Preserve ALEFIT’s existing logo and recognizable lime/green brand accent.
- Use mostly neutral dark charcoal surfaces, readable white text, and subtle secondary text.
- Use the accent selectively for important actions and selected states.
- Avoid green-tinted backgrounds, giant neon headlines, heavy gradients, excessive borders, and unnecessary decorations.
- Use consistent typography, spacing, card shapes, buttons, and icons.
- Avoid oversized all-caps headings, long promotional paragraphs, feature lists above the actual activity, and cards nested inside more cards.
- Make the primary action immediately obvious.
- Reveal secondary information when requested instead of showing everything at once.
- Build responsive layouts for phones, tablets, and desktop.
- Account for iPhone safe areas, the keyboard, and bottom navigation.
- Use accessible contrast, labeled controls, visible keyboard focus, and comfortable touch targets.

Changing colors alone is not enough. Fix the information hierarchy and interactions.

CORE NAVIGATION

Use four primary sections:
1. Today
2. My Plan
3. Progress
4. Profile

On mobile, use persistent bottom navigation.
On desktop, adapt this into a clean navigation layout.

Preserve navigation state appropriately. Browser Back and direct links should work.

MY PLAN

- Show a weekly schedule with clear training and recovery days.
- Let users select a date and inspect the session.
- Support browsing previous and upcoming weeks.
- Clearly distinguish completed sessions, saved past sessions, and future previews.
- Preserve what actually happened in completed workouts.
- Never regenerate a historical workout and present it as what the user completed.
- Explain that upcoming sessions may adjust with feedback.
- Make training preferences easy to edit.
- When preferences change, update future programming without rewriting completed history.

Do not present every sport as another generic program to buy or choose.

Provide thoughtful empty states for new users.

Do not fabricate strength increases, estimated transformations, calorie burns, streaks, personal records, or performance percentages.

Separate workout attendance from actual performance improvement.

PERSONALIZATION BEFORE SIGNUP

Do not ask for name, email, and password first.

Organize setup into clear stages:
Your goals → Your routine → Your plan → Save.

Use approximately six short question screens, followed by the plan preview and account creation.

Suggested questions:

1. What are you training for?
   Choose a sport or general fitness.
   Include basketball, soccer, football, baseball/softball, tennis, volleyball, running/track, swimming, combat sports, cycling, strength training, and other sports where properly supported.
   Allow optional position or event details.

2. What would you like to improve?
   Tailor the choices to the selected sport.
   Examples: strength, muscle, speed, jumping, endurance, mobility, general fitness.
   For basketball, use understandable options such as jump higher, quicker first step, and late-game stamina.
   Let users choose up to three priorities.

3. What is your experience?
   Beginner, intermediate, advanced.
   Explain these briefly and avoid assuming advanced ability.

4. What equipment can you use?
   Full gym, dumbbells, bands, bodyweight, field/court/track, pool, and other supported equipment.
   Ensure conflicting choices are handled sensibly.

5. When can you train?
   Available days and usual session duration.
   Keep rest and recovery realistic.
   Where needed, account for existing sports practices or games without turning onboarding into a long interview.

6. Anything the plan should account for?
   Age range and optional pain or movement limitations.
   Explain why this information matters.
   Collect only what is necessary.

- Large, obvious answer choices.
- Short useful copy.
- Clear progress.
- Back navigation that retains answers.
- Explicit selection states.
- No fake loading process, countdown, testimonials, or unsupported results promises.
- Avoid unnecessary typed answers.
- Keep optional details optional.

PLAN REVEAL

Before requiring an account, show genuine value:

- A concise personalized summary.
- The first workout.
- A preview of the week.
- Recovery days.
- A short explanation of how their answers affected the plan.

For example, show that a basketball plan fits three available days and dumbbells—but only if those are the user’s actual answers.

Lead with the useful result, not a wall of explanation.

Allow individual answers to be edited directly from the preview. Users should not have to repeat every question.

Then show:
“Save my plan and start today.”

After signup, take the user directly into their saved personalized experience.

AUTHENTICATION

Audit and repair the existing signup and login flow.

- Use the project’s supported secure authentication approach.
- Do not replace working authentication unnecessarily.
- Support signup, login, logout, session restoration, and password reset.
- Provide meaningful errors for invalid inputs, duplicate accounts, incorrect credentials, expired sessions, and network problems.
- Prevent duplicate submissions.
- Handle partial success: if the account was created but the plan failed to save, retry saving the plan without creating another account.
- Preserve onboarding answers during recoverable failures.
- Keep tokens and secrets out of URLs and logs wherever possible.
- Validate redirect destinations.
- Enforce user ownership on the server.
- Never rely solely on hiding UI for authorization.
- Make password recovery work end to end, or clearly report the missing email configuration.
- Do not claim login can “never fail.”

PERSONALIZED TRAINING ENGINE

Make the programming genuinely depend on:
- Sport.
- Goals.
- Experience.
- Age-appropriate considerations.
- Equipment.
- Available training days.
- Session duration.
- Limitations.
- Existing sport workload where supplied.
- Recent workout completion.
- Energy, soreness, and difficulty feedback.

Do not simply change the sport name on the same workout.

Account for different demands such as:
- Basketball: acceleration, deceleration, lateral movement, jumping, strength, and repeat-effort conditioning.
- Running: appropriate running volume, intensity distribution, strength, and recovery.
- General strength: progressive resistance training and balanced movement patterns.
- Other supported sports: relevant demands with suitable exercise selection.

Where support is limited, be honest. Do not claim expert programming for every possible sport.

Use a structured, testable planning system with exercise metadata and sensible constraints.

WORKOUT CONTENT

Each session should explain:
- Warm-up.
- Exercises or drills.
- Sets and repetitions, time, or distance.
- Rest periods.
- Effort guidance.
- Technique cues.
- Suitable substitutions.
- Cool-down or recovery work when appropriate.

Workout duration must reasonably match the requested time.

Do not recommend equipment the user does not have.

Do not stack demanding sessions without considering recovery and sports practice.

WORKOUT EXECUTION

Build a focused session view:
- Clear current exercise or section.
- Easy navigation through exercises.
- Readable prescriptions and cues.
- Exercise completion tracking.
- Logging of relevant reps, weights, times, or distances.
- A rest timer where useful.
- A clear finish action.

Persist meaningful workout progress so accidental navigation or refreshing does not lose recorded work.

After the workout, ask a short difficulty question:
Too easy / Just right / Too hard.

Save completion reliably and update Progress immediately.

Prevent duplicate workout or feedback records on repeated taps or retries.

DAILY ADAPTATION

Keep the readiness check-in short:
- Energy.
- Soreness.
- Time available.

Use it to make sensible adjustments:
- Reduce volume when needed.
- Shorten a session when time is limited.
- Substitute appropriate exercises.
- Recommend recovery rather than forcing hard training.
- Progress gradually when the user is consistently ready.

Explain important changes in plain language.

Use trends across sessions rather than overreacting to one answer.

REST DAYS

Give clear, optional recovery guidance:
- Rest or easy activity.
- Gentle mobility where appropriate.
- Basic recovery reminders.

Do not turn every recovery day into another demanding workout.

Make it clear that pain is not the same as ordinary training soreness. Avoid diagnosis or treatment claims. Recommend qualified guidance when necessary.

For younger users, use conservative, age-appropriate guidance. Do not recommend aggressive weight loss or unsafe maximal training.

AI EXPECTATIONS

I want this to be very intelligent, but do not fake intelligence.

- Clearly distinguish deterministic programming rules from an actual language-model integration.
- Use structured workout data and validation.
- If an LLM is used, call it server-side.
- Keep API keys secret.
- Validate outputs for equipment, duration, workload, and safety constraints.
- Provide a reliable fallback if the model is unavailable.
- Do not let an AI response bypass safety or programming rules.
- Do not promise unlimited free AI usage.
- Explain any required provider configuration and costs before adding paid dependencies.
- The app should remain useful without a chatbot.
